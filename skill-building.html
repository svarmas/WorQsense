Training for Investigators and Decision-Makers

Most compliance training explains obligations. Ours develops investigative capability. These programmes are designed for people responsible for conducting inquiries, assessing evidence, making findings, managing investigations and writing reports that will withstand scrutiny.

Internal Committee Training

For IC members and Chairpersons

End-to-end investigative capability for Internal Committee members conducting PoSH Act inquiries. Built around what actually goes wrong in IC investigations: poor scoping, unstructured interviews, weak credibility assessment and findings that cannot be defended on appeal. Grounded in natural justice principles and Indian legal obligations.

  • –Complaint receipt, preliminary assessment and scoping
  • –Structured interviewing: complainant, respondent and witnesses
  • –Evidence assessment and credibility analysis
  • –Standard of proof and making defensible findings
  • –Report writing that holds up under review

GRC Investigation Training

For Grievance Redressal Committee members

Structured investigative capability for GRC members conducting inquiries under the Industrial Relations Code, 2020. The IR Code imposes grievance handling obligations that demand the same procedural rigour as PoSH. This programme builds that capability, specifically for GRC contexts.

  • –IR Code obligations and GRC responsibilities
  • –Natural justice: notice, opportunity to respond, fairness
  • –Evidence gathering and assessment in employment contexts
  • –Investigative decision-making and defensible findings
  • –Practical scenarios: live GRC case exercises

Investigative Interviewing for Auditors

In partnership with IMS Australia

Audit findings depend on what people say. Most auditors are not trained to elicit it.

Internal auditors, compliance officers, forensic auditors and external auditors routinely conduct interviews as part of their work. Those interviews are often the most consequential part of the inquiry. This programme applies structured investigative interviewing methodology to the audit context: how to plan an interview, build rapport without compromising objectivity, manage accounts, probe for detail and document accurately. Delivered with Interview Management Solutions, Australia.

  • –Interview planning and objective-setting in the audit context
  • –Rapport, neutrality and the risk of leading questions
  • –Free recall, account management and clarification technique
  • –Handling evasion, inconsistency and partial disclosure
  • –Documentation that supports findings and withstands challenge

For internal auditors, compliance and ethics officers, forensic auditors and external audit teams.

Courts no longer ask if you had a policy.
They ask if your process would withstand scrutiny.

Weak investigations are now a documented liability. Regulators and courts are examining process quality, not just whether a policy exists.

Scrutiny is rising

PEACE Model

The internationally recognised structure for ethical, evidence-based investigative interviews: Preparation and Planning, Engage and Explain, Account, Closure, Evaluate.

Méndez Principles

Non-coercive, rights-respecting interview practice per the UN Special Rapporteur's framework on effective interviewing for investigations and intelligence gathering.

TILES System®

IMS's structured interview planning and record-keeping system. Pre-interview planning, in-interview execution, post-interview evaluation, logged as the interview happens.

In partnership with Interview Management Solutions (IMS, Australia). Led by Daren Jay: 30+ years in law enforcement investigations, UK Home Office Investigative Interview Trainer, lecturer at Charles Sturt University.

Manager training

The First Response Often Shapes the Entire Outcome

Managers are not investigators. They do not conduct inquiries. But how a manager responds in the first hours and days of a workplace concern, whether they receive a disclosure appropriately, handle difficult conversations with care, escalate correctly and avoid actions that could constitute retaliation, determines what the investigation team inherits. This programme builds that confidence.

Recognising concerns early

Identifying conduct concerns and informal complaints before they escalate, and understanding when a formal pathway is required.

Receiving a disclosure

What to say, what not to say, and how to respond in a way that does not compromise the subsequent process or the person disclosing.

Difficult conversations

Addressing conduct concerns directly, without prejudging an outcome or creating a record that undermines a future inquiry.

Escalation and retaliation

Escalation responsibilities under PoSH and the IR Code, and how to manage the period between complaint and conclusion without creating retaliation risk.
