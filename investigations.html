The decision in front of you

When the matter should not be investigated internally.

Some matters cannot be handled by the people who would normally handle them. Not because the internal team lacks integrity, but because of who is involved, who knows whom, and how the process will look when someone examines it later.

A senior leader is involved

The reporting lines run through the person whose conduct is in question.

The internal team knows the parties

Proximity is a fact, and it will be raised.

Independence could reasonably be questioned

A sound process can still be undone by who ran it.

Internal capacity is untested

The Internal Committee or ER team holds the mandate but has not run an inquiry like this before. The 90-day PoSH deadline does not wait for that experience to be built.

An earlier process is being questioned

A completed investigation is now under scrutiny, before a tribunal, on appeal, or by the board, and someone needs to assess it honestly.

If more than one of these is true, the question is no longer whether to bring in an independent investigator. It is who.

The inflection point

Why this matters now.

PoSH obligations maturing

After a decade, process quality is drawing more attention, not just compliance on paper.

GRC mandate under the new labour codes

The Industrial Relations Code extends structured grievance handling to a far broader class of employers. The obligation is coming before most organisations are ready.

The 90-day deadline is unforgiving

The PoSH Act requires inquiries to be completed within 90 days. Most IC members have never conducted an inquiry before.

Improvisation carries real risk

Ad-hoc responses create legal, ethical, and reputational exposure. Good intentions are not a substitute for process.

Every engagement

Independent. Structured. Evidence-led. Human.

Independent means being free to reach the finding the evidence supports, including an unwelcome one.

Independent

No advisory role, no prior relationship, no conflicting position.

Structured

Procedural fairness at every step: allegations put clearly, a real opportunity to respond, a record of how each decision was reached.

Evidence-led

Findings tied to evidence. Inferences documented as inferences. Credibility assessed on reasoning that can be read and tested.

Human

Trauma-aware practice that accounts for memory, disclosure, and credibility. Honest scope, proportionate conclusions, no inflated findings.

Where evidence arising during an investigation identifies relevant issues outside the terms of reference, these can be identified separately for the organisation's consideration.

How an investigation runs

From first call to final report.

Step 1

Scope and terms of reference

What is being investigated, against what standard, and within what boundaries. Agreed in writing before work begins.

Step 2

Evidence gathering and interviews

Structured interviews with complainant, respondent, and witnesses, to PEACE model and Méndez Principles standards. Documentary evidence reviewed in parallel.

Step 3

Analysis and findings

Evidence weighed, credibility assessed, and findings reached against the applicable standard of proof. Reasoning set out, not just conclusions.

Step 4

Reporting

A written report proportionate to the matter: facts found, reasoning, and findings, delivered within the agreed timeline.
